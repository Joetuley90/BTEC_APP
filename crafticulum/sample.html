Teaching content

Teach · 10 min · D.P5

What performance data tells you

Performance data is any information that describes how you played. Some of it is numbers the game records for you, such as kills, deaths and assists, accuracy, and time spent on the objective. Some of it is footage: a VOD is a recording of the match that you watch back. Numbers tell you what happened. Footage tells you why it happened. Coaches use both together, because a number on its own can mislead. A support player can have a low KDA and still be the reason the team won.

  • KDA sums up kills, deaths and assists. A common formula is (kills + assists) ÷ deaths
  • Accuracy is the percentage of your shots that hit
  • Objective time is how long you spent on the point, payload or site
  • A VOD review means watching the match back to see the decisions behind the numbers

KDA Kills, deaths and assists, often shown as a ratioVOD Video on demand: a recording of a match you can watch backScrim A practice match arranged against another team

Ask the class: Why might the player with the most kills still be the reason their team lost?

Teach · 10 min · D.P6

Turning data into strengths and weaknesses

One match proves very little. Anyone can have a good or a bad game, so analysts look for patterns: the same result in two or more matches. A strength is something the data shows you do well again and again. A weakness is something that costs your team again and again. Every claim needs evidence, which means a number or a timestamp from the VOD. Then ask what the weakness costs the team. Dying first in most fights matters more than slightly low accuracy, because the team has to fight with a player missing.

  • Look for patterns across at least two matches, not one-offs
  • Back every strength and weakness with a number or a VOD timestamp
  • Judge a weakness by what it costs the team, not by how bad it looks
  • Compare like with like: same game, same role, similar opponents

Pattern The same result showing up in more than one matchEvidence The number, screenshot or timestamp that proves your pointImpact The effect on your team's chance of winning

Ask the class: Your accuracy was 55% in one match and 31% in the next. Is accuracy a weakness?

Tasks

Task · 15 min · D.P5

Know your data types

A labelled list of 4 types of performance data and what each one shows

  1. Copy the 4 headings from the board: KDA, accuracy, objective time, VOD review
  2. Under each heading, write one sentence on what it measures
  3. Add one reason a coach would use it
  4. Highlight the one you think matters most for your role

Stretch: Explain one limit of each data type, e.g. KDA ignores support play.

Task · 35 min · D.P6 / D.M4

Analyse your own matches

A completed strengths and weaknesses table using stats from 2 of your matches

  1. Open your last 2 ranked or scrim matches in the stats tracker
  2. Record kills, deaths, assists, accuracy and objective time in the table
  3. Screenshot both scoreboards and paste them under the table
  4. Write 2 strengths and 2 weaknesses, each backed by a number
  5. Save as Unit2_LAD_YourName

Stretch: Link each weakness to a moment in your VOD with a timestamp.

Task · 15 min · CD.D3

Rank your improvements

A ranked list of 3 improvements with a justified top choice

  1. List 3 things you could improve from your table
  2. Rank them by how much they would help your team win
  3. Write a paragraph justifying your number 1 choice
  4. Suggest one training drill for it

Stretch: Compare your number 1 with a pro player's stats in the same role.

Exit quiz

Have a go. In class, learners answer this on their phones from a QR code and you see who got what.

1. What does KDA stand for?
It sums up a player's kills, deaths and assists.
2. Why review VOD footage as well as stats?
Stats show what happened, VOD shows the decisions behind it.
3. Which is the best evidence of a weakness?
A pattern across matches is more reliable than a one-off.
4. What makes an improvement target strong?
Good targets are evidenced and focused on winning impact.
