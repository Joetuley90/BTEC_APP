Students

Students don't have accounts and aren't asked for an email address.

WhatWhy
The name a student types when they join a quiz, an assessment, a live lesson or a class pageSo their teacher knows whose answers they are
Their answers, scores and progress through a lesson's tasksSo the teacher can see how the class is doing and what to go over again
Where Second Marker is used: which criteria a marked piece of work met, with no name or ID attachedSo a department's leaders can see what groups need more teaching on

The admin of this site chooses how long students' names and answers are kept, from 30 days to a year, or until they are deleted by hand. Progress during a live lesson is cleared after a day. Class scores kept for results pages are percentages with no names.

If you contact us

When you use the contact page we keep your name, your email address and your message so we can reply, along with the kind of browser you used. If you were signed in, it also notes your role and department. Messages are kept on this site until the person who looks after it deletes them, and a copy is emailed to them.

Where it's kept

Everything above is kept on the web server this site runs on, which is chosen and controlled by the organisation that runs it. Backups, where they're switched on, are kept on the same server.

The lawful basis

For a school or college, this is usually the public task of providing education. For other organisations it is usually legitimate interests in planning and delivering teaching. Staff account details are also needed to provide the account.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the information held about you, have it corrected or deleted, object to how it's used, or ask for a copy. Staff can change their own details and delete their account's sign-in methods in Settings. A student, or their parent or carer, can ask their teacher to delete their answers.

To use any of these rights, or to ask a question, contact .

To use any of these rights, contact the organisation that runs this site, through your teacher or head of department.

If you're not happy with the answer, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
